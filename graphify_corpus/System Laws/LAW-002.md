# LAW-002
# Laws of Reasoning

**Status:** Canonical  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Classification:** Foundational Law

---

# Purpose

This document establishes the immutable laws governing reasoning within the Creative Intelligence Platform.

Reasoning is the process that transforms verified knowledge into decisions.

These laws apply to every reasoning engine, AI agent, workflow, recommendation system, planning engine, and orchestration service.

Implementations may evolve.

The laws governing reasoning must remain stable.

---

# Fundamental Principle

> **Reasoning is the disciplined transformation of knowledge into justified decisions.**

Reasoning is neither guessing nor memorisation.

A conclusion is valid only when it can be explained, justified, and traced back to verified knowledge.

# Definitions

## Interpretation

Applying context to existing knowledge.

Interpretation gives knowledge situational meaning.

---

## Inference

A logical conclusion derived from one or more verified knowledge claims.

Inference creates new understanding without inventing unsupported facts.

---

## Hypothesis

A provisional explanation or recommendation awaiting validation.

Hypotheses are not knowledge.

---

## Recommendation

A proposed course of action supported by reasoning.

Recommendations remain optional until accepted or executed.

---

## Decision

A selected course of action.

Decisions may be made by:

- Human
- Platform
- Shared collaboration

Ownership must always be explicit.

# The Twelve Laws of Reasoning

---

## Law 1 — Reasoning Begins With Knowledge

Reasoning may only begin from verified knowledge or explicitly identified assumptions.

Unsupported assumptions must never be presented as facts.

---

## Law 2 — Context Governs Meaning

The same knowledge may produce different conclusions under different contexts.

Reasoning must always consider:

- Brand
- Product
- Audience
- Market
- Campaign Objective
- Intelligence Domain
- Time

Reasoning without context is incomplete.

---

## Law 3 — Every Conclusion Requires Justification

Every recommendation must answer:

- Why this?
- Why now?
- Why not an alternative?

A conclusion without justification is not reasoning.

---

## Law 4 — Uncertainty Must Be Preserved

Reasoning must never convert uncertainty into certainty.

When evidence is incomplete, the platform should:

- lower confidence
- request clarification
- present alternatives
- defer commitment

Honest uncertainty is preferable to false confidence.

---

## Law 5 — Multiple Valid Solutions May Exist

Creative problems rarely have a single correct answer.

The platform should explore multiple viable approaches before favouring one.

Diversity is a feature of creative reasoning.

---

## Law 6 — Human Intent Has Priority

Business Intent provided by the user is authoritative.

Reasoning exists to satisfy intent, not replace it.

If reasoning conflicts with declared objectives, the platform should explain the conflict rather than silently override user intent.

---

## Law 7 — Recommendations Precede Automation

The platform should recommend before acting whenever decisions materially affect creative direction.

Automation is earned through demonstrated trust.

---

## Law 8 — Every Reasoning Step Is Traceable

Each conclusion should be connected to:

- supporting knowledge
- intermediate inferences
- confidence
- decision owner

Reasoning must form an auditable chain.

---

## Law 9 — Reasoning Must Optimise for Coherence

Creative decisions should reinforce one another.

Examples include:

- positioning
- typography
- colour
- photography
- messaging
- composition

Local optimisation must never damage overall campaign coherence.

---

## Law 10 — Outcomes Validate Reasoning

Reasoning is evaluated by outcomes.

Successful outcomes strengthen confidence.

Poor outcomes trigger review and refinement.

Learning follows evaluation.

---

## Law 11 — Reasoning Must Be Reproducible

Given the same:

- knowledge
- context
- objectives
- constraints

the platform should produce substantially similar reasoning.

Randomness may create variation in execution, but not inconsistency in logic.

---

## Law 12 — Reasoning Exists to Improve Human Judgement

The platform should amplify human judgement rather than replace it.

The measure of successful reasoning is not autonomous behaviour.

It is better human decisions.

---

# Reasoning Integrity Rules

The platform must reject reasoning that:

- contradicts verified knowledge without evidence
- ignores relevant context
- hides uncertainty
- cannot explain its conclusions
- violates declared business intent
- depends on unverifiable assumptions

Integrity is more valuable than speed.

---

# Confidence Model

Every reasoning outcome carries confidence.

Confidence is influenced by:

- quality of knowledge
- completeness of context
- strength of evidence
- historical performance
- agreement between reasoning pathways

Confidence expresses certainty, not correctness.

---

# Alternative Generation

For significant creative decisions, the platform should generate multiple plausible options before selecting a recommendation.

Each alternative should explain:

- strengths
- weaknesses
- assumptions
- expected trade-offs

Users should understand why one option is preferred.

---

# Explainability

Every major recommendation should expose:

- Inputs
- Relevant Knowledge
- Reasoning Path
- Assumptions
- Confidence
- Expected Outcome

Explainability is a first-class capability, not an optional feature.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge

Knowledge answers:

> "What is known?"

Reasoning answers:

> "Given what is known, what should we do?"

Knowledge provides facts.

Reasoning provides direction.

---

# Relationship to Memory

Memory influences reasoning by providing experience.

Memory does not replace knowledge.

Past success should inform future decisions without becoming unquestioned truth.

---

# Relationship to Execution

Reasoning determines:

- what should happen

Execution determines:

- how it happens

The platform must keep these responsibilities distinct.

---

# Immutable Principles

Reasoning should always be:

- Knowledge-driven
- Context-aware
- Explainable
- Traceable
- Evidence-based
- Collaborative
- Coherent
- Adaptable

These principles apply regardless of implementation.

---

# Final Law

> **Every recommendation, decision, and creative conclusion within the Creative Intelligence Platform must be explainable, context-aware, evidence-backed, traceable, and aligned with explicit human intent.**

If a conclusion cannot satisfy these conditions, it should remain a hypothesis rather than become a recommendation.

---

# Status

**LAW-002 is the constitutional foundation of reasoning within the Creative Intelligence Platform.**

All reasoning engines, planning systems, recommendation services, AI agents, orchestration pipelines, and collaborative workflows must comply with these laws before producing decisions or recommendations.
