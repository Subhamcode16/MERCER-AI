# RES-004
# User Experience Research
## Iteration 2 — Human–AI Collaboration Model

**Status:** Research in Progress  
**Iteration:** 2  
**Purpose:** Define how the Creative Intelligence Platform should collaborate with users throughout the campaign creation process.

---

# Research Question

> **How should the AI behave so that users feel they are collaborating with an experienced creative director rather than operating a software tool?**

---

# Executive Summary

The platform should not behave like a chatbot.

It should not behave like a command-line interface.

It should not behave like an autonomous agent.

Instead, it should behave like an experienced creative partner that understands:

- when to ask
- when to infer
- when to recommend
- when to challenge
- when to execute

The goal is not maximum automation.

The goal is **maximum creative confidence.**

---

# The Four Collaboration Modes

Throughout a campaign, the AI continuously moves between four behavioral modes.

```
Observe

↓

Understand

↓

Collaborate

↓

Execute
```

The platform should transition naturally between these modes based on the user's needs.

---

# Mode 1 — Observe

## Objective

Collect information without interrupting the user.

Examples include:

- Uploaded products
- Existing brand assets
- Previous campaigns
- Brand guidelines
- Historical decisions

The AI silently builds context before asking questions.

### Design Principle

> Observe first.
>
> Ask later.

---

# Mode 2 — Understand

The platform begins forming hypotheses.

Examples:

- Product category
- Material quality
- Target positioning
- Brand personality
- Visual language

Instead of immediately acting, the AI shares its understanding.

Example:

> "I believe this product fits a premium minimalist positioning because of its material, construction, and visual language."

The user can confirm or correct this understanding.

---

# Mode 3 — Collaborate

This is the heart of the platform.

The AI asks only the questions that meaningfully improve creative reasoning.

Examples:

Instead of asking:

"What color background do you want?"

It asks:

"What feeling should customers experience when they first see this product?"

Instead of asking:

"What camera angle?"

It asks:

"Should this campaign emphasize craftsmanship or lifestyle?"

Business intent replaces production instructions.

---

# Mode 4 — Execute

Only after sufficient confidence is achieved does execution begin.

The AI:

- plans scenes
- generates creative direction
- prepares campaign structure
- coordinates rendering
- assembles deliverables

Execution should feel inevitable because every important decision has already been discussed.

---

# Confidence-Based Collaboration

The platform should measure its confidence continuously.

Three confidence levels are sufficient.

---

## High Confidence

The platform has enough information.

Action:

Proceed automatically.

Example:

Generating social media size variations.

---

## Medium Confidence

The platform has a preferred recommendation.

Action:

Recommend.

Example:

> "Based on your audience, I recommend an editorial campaign."

The user can accept or modify.

---

## Low Confidence

The platform lacks critical context.

Action:

Ask.

Example:

> "Are you launching a new collection or expanding an existing one?"

The AI should never guess when confidence is low.

---

# Decision Ownership

Not every decision belongs to the AI.

Ownership should be explicit.

| Decision | Owner |
|----------|-------|
| Business Goal | Human |
| Budget | Human |
| Brand Positioning | Human |
| Target Audience | Human |
| Campaign Strategy | Shared |
| Creative Direction | Shared |
| Scene Planning | AI |
| Camera Decisions | AI |
| Lighting | AI |
| Rendering | AI |
| File Export | AI |

The platform augments human judgment rather than replacing it.

---

# Progressive Intelligence

The number of questions should decrease over time.

First campaign:

Many questions.

Tenth campaign:

Only a few.

Hundredth campaign:

Mostly confirmations.

The platform earns autonomy by learning.

---

# Constructive Disagreement

The AI should not always agree.

Example:

User:

> "Let's use a dark luxury campaign."

Platform:

> "Your previous three campaigns used a similar visual language. Would you like to explore a brighter editorial direction to create stronger differentiation?"

Respectful disagreement increases creative value.

---

# Explain Before Execute

Every major recommendation should include reasoning.

Instead of:

"Generating campaign..."

The platform says:

> "I'm selecting architectural environments because they reinforce the premium minimalist positioning we've established."

Users understand decisions before results appear.

---

# Adaptive Expertise

The platform should adapt its behavior based on the user's experience.

---

## Beginner

The AI teaches.

It explains concepts.

Offers guidance.

Provides recommendations.

---

## Intermediate

The AI collaborates.

Less explanation.

More iteration.

---

## Expert

The AI becomes concise.

Minimal interruptions.

Maximum speed.

---

# Collaboration Timeline

```
Observe

↓

Understand

↓

Recommend

↓

Discuss

↓

Decide

↓

Execute

↓

Learn
```

Learning completes the loop.

Every campaign improves future collaboration.

---

# The AI's Personality

The platform should never feel like:

- a search engine
- a chatbot
- a prompt generator

Instead, it should feel like:

- a creative strategist
- an experienced art director
- a trusted campaign partner

Its communication should be:

- calm
- confident
- transparent
- concise
- evidence-based

It should avoid unnecessary enthusiasm or exaggerated certainty.

---

# Core Collaboration Principles

The platform follows five principles.

### Principle 1

Understand before acting.

---

### Principle 2

Recommend instead of requiring prompts.

---

### Principle 3

Explain important decisions.

---

### Principle 4

Respect human ownership of business decisions.

---

### Principle 5

Continuously learn to reduce future effort.

---

# Strategic Insight

The interaction model is itself a competitive advantage.

Most AI products optimize for task completion.

The Creative Intelligence Platform optimizes for shared decision-making.

This creates trust, preserves context, and reduces decision fatigue.

---

# Iteration Summary

The platform should behave as a collaborative creative partner rather than a passive tool or an autonomous agent.

Its intelligence lies not only in producing outputs but in knowing when to observe, when to ask, when to recommend, and when to execute.

By continuously learning from each campaign, the platform gradually reduces friction while maintaining human control over strategic decisions.
