# Library 1 — Textile Encyclopedia

This library serves as the Product DNA foundation, defining the structural and tactile properties of various fabrics.

## Silk
* **Weave:** Plain weave, velvet, chenille, chiffon, corduroy, crepe, jersey, knit, lace, organza, plush, satin, serge, taffeta, tulle, tweed, and patterned silks on draw-looms.
* **Weight:** Not specified in sources.
* **Stiffness:** Not specified in sources.
* **Transparency:** Not specified in sources.
* **Drape:** Not specified in sources.
* **Reflectivity:** Not specified in sources.
* **Wrinkle behavior:** Not specified in sources.
* **Texture:** Velvet variants feature a short pile.
* **Embroidery compatibility:** Compatible with chain stitch, ari embroidery, and silk thread.
* **Common use cases:** Courtly clothing, furnishings, saris, skirts, and jackets.
* **Premium vs budget variants:** Not specified in sources.

## Cotton
* **Weave:** Plain weave (Khadi), canvas, chambray, chenille, chiffon, corduroy, denim, flannel, gingham, jersey, knit, lace, lawn, plush, tulle, twill, and velvet.
* **Weight:** Ranges from extremely fine to extremely rough.
* **Stiffness:** Not specified in sources.
* **Transparency:** Not specified in sources.
* **Drape:** Not specified in sources.
* **Reflectivity:** Not specified in sources.
* **Wrinkle behavior:** Not specified in sources.
* **Texture:** Ranges from extremely fine to extremely rough.
* **Embroidery compatibility:** Compatible with painted dyes, block printing, and ink or gold paint.
* **Common use cases:** Dress fabrics, furnishings, tents, everyday utilitarian textiles, tailored garments, accessories, sail-cloth, muslins, chintzes, handkerchiefs, ginghams, and calicos.
* **Premium vs budget variants:** Luxury muslins and chintzes versus inexpensive handkerchiefs, ginghams, and calicos.
