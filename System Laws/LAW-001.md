# LAW-001
# Laws of Knowledge

**Status:** Canonical  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Classification:** Foundational Law

---

# Purpose

This document establishes the immutable laws governing knowledge within the Creative Intelligence Platform.

Knowledge is the platform's most valuable asset.

Every reasoning engine, memory system, ontology, knowledge graph, AI agent, workflow, database, API, and user interaction must conform to these laws.

These laws are implementation-independent.

Technology may evolve.

These laws should not.

---

# Fundamental Principle

> **Knowledge is not information.**

Information becomes knowledge only after it satisfies the laws defined in this document.

The platform must distinguish between:

- Data
- Information
- Knowledge
- Wisdom

Failure to distinguish these concepts leads to unreliable reasoning and degraded intelligence.

# Definitions

## Observation

A raw fact captured from the world.

Examples:

- An uploaded image.
- A product description.
- A user statement.
- A sales metric.

Observations are not interpreted.

---

## Data

Structured representation of observations.

Examples:

- RGB values
- Product dimensions
- Price
- Category

Data has structure but not meaning.

---

## Information

Data interpreted within context.

Example:

"The product is made of full-grain leather."

Information carries meaning but has not yet been validated for reuse.

---

## Knowledge

Verified information that can be reliably reused across future reasoning tasks.

Knowledge is persistent.

Knowledge survives beyond the task that created it.

---

## Wisdom

Reliable decision-making developed through accumulated knowledge and experience over time.

Wisdom is an emergent property of the platform.

It is never entered manually.

# The Twelve Laws of Knowledge

---

## Law 1 — Knowledge Must Originate From Evidence

No knowledge may exist without supporting evidence.

Evidence may include:

- User-provided information
- Product images
- Product documentation
- Historical campaigns
- Verified datasets
- Measured outcomes

Every knowledge claim must reference its origin.

Nothing becomes knowledge because the system "believes" it.

---

## Law 2 — Every Knowledge Claim Has Provenance

Every knowledge claim must record:

- Source
- Time of creation
- Creator (human or system)
- Supporting evidence
- Confidence
- Version

Knowledge without provenance is untrustworthy.

---

## Law 3 — Knowledge Is Versioned

Knowledge is never silently overwritten.

Instead:

```
Old Version

↓

Revision

↓

New Version
```

Historical versions remain accessible.

The platform preserves intellectual history.

---

## Law 4 — Knowledge Is Contextual

Knowledge is only valid within its context.

Examples:

A campaign strategy may be correct for:

- one brand
- one audience
- one season

It must not automatically become universal knowledge.

Context is part of the knowledge itself.

---

## Law 5 — Knowledge Is Explicit

Reasoning must depend on explicit knowledge rather than hidden assumptions.

If the platform cannot explain why it knows something,

it should not behave as though it knows it.

Explainability is a prerequisite for trust.

---

## Law 6 — Knowledge Must Be Traceable

Every conclusion must be traceable back through intermediate reasoning to supporting evidence.

Users should be able to answer:

- Why?
- Based on what?
- From where?

No conclusion should exist without an auditable chain of reasoning.

---

## Law 7 — Knowledge Is Reusable

Knowledge should be represented independently of the task that created it.

Example:

Understanding that a brand prefers restrained typography should inform future campaigns, not remain isolated within a single project.

Knowledge gains value through reuse.

---

## Law 8 — Knowledge Is Connected

Knowledge never exists in isolation.

Every knowledge entity should participate in a semantic network.

Relationships are first-class citizens.

Examples:

- Product belongs to Brand.
- Campaign promotes Product.
- Creative Direction implements Campaign Strategy.

The platform reasons over relationships as much as entities.

---

## Law 9 — Knowledge Evolves

Knowledge is expected to improve over time.

New evidence may:

- strengthen
- refine
- qualify
- replace
- invalidate

existing knowledge.

Change is a feature, not a failure.

---

## Law 10 — Knowledge Has Confidence

Every knowledge claim carries an explicit confidence estimate.

Confidence reflects the reliability of the claim based on available evidence.

Confidence is not truth.

It is a measure of certainty.

The platform should expose confidence when it materially affects decisions.

---

## Law 11 — Knowledge Must Be Minimal

Store the smallest reusable unit of knowledge.

Avoid embedding multiple independent facts into a single claim.

Smaller knowledge units are easier to:

- validate
- update
- retrieve
- reason over
- reuse

Atomic knowledge produces scalable intelligence.

---

## Law 12 — Knowledge Exists to Improve Decisions

Knowledge is not collected for archival purposes alone.

Its purpose is to improve future reasoning and decision-making.

Knowledge that never contributes to better outcomes has limited value.

  confidence:

This structure is implementation-neutral.

Specific storage technologies may vary while preserving these semantics.

---

# Knowledge Integrity Rules

The platform should reject knowledge that:

- lacks evidence
- contradicts verified knowledge without justification
- cannot be traced to a source
- has no meaningful context
- duplicates an existing claim unnecessarily

Integrity is more valuable than quantity.

---

# Knowledge Quality Dimensions

Every knowledge claim may be evaluated across these dimensions:

- Accuracy
- Completeness
- Consistency
- Traceability
- Relevance
- Freshness
- Explainability
- Reusability

These dimensions guide continuous improvement.

# Relationship to Other Systems

These laws govern all persistent knowledge used by:

- Knowledge Graph
- Memory Framework
- Creative Intelligence Engine
- AI Collaboration System
- Campaign Planning
- Product Understanding
- Brand Understanding
- Domain Intelligence
- Recommendation Engine
- Evaluation Framework

No subsystem is exempt.

---

# Relationship to Memory

Knowledge and Memory serve different purposes.

| Knowledge | Memory |
|-----------|--------|
| Represents verified facts | Represents accumulated experience |
| Requires evidence | Requires historical interaction |
| Changes through validation | Changes through reinforcement |
| Optimized for reasoning | Optimized for adaptation |
| Shared across the system | May be entity-specific or user-specific |

Knowledge explains **what is known**.

Memory preserves **what has been experienced**.

---

# Relationship to Reasoning

Knowledge is the input.

Reasoning is the process.

Decisions are the output.

The platform must never confuse these responsibilities.

---

# Immutable Principle

> **A system that stores more information is not necessarily more intelligent.**

A system becomes more intelligent when it stores better knowledge, understands relationships between that knowledge, and applies it to improve future decisions.

---

# Final Law

> **Every persistent fact within the Creative Intelligence Platform must exist as a verifiable, contextual, traceable, versioned, evidence-backed knowledge claim.**

If a fact cannot satisfy these conditions, it should remain information rather than become knowledge.

---

# Status

**LAW-001 is the constitutional foundation of knowledge within the Creative Intelligence Platform.**

Every future specification, ontology, memory framework, database schema, API, reasoning engine, and AI agent must comply with these laws before persistent knowledge is created or consumed.
