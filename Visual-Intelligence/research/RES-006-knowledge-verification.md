# RES-006: Knowledge Verification (Processing Station 6)

## Purpose

**Processing Station 6 (Knowledge Verification)** is the Continuous Learning Engine of the Visual Intelligence Factory (VIF).

The fundamental challenge of building an AI knowledge base is poisoned data. A single hallucinated tag (e.g., classifying a polyester blend as pure Banarasi Silk) corrupts the downstream knowledge graph. 

To solve this, Station 6 intercepts the array of `Knowledge Claims` generated by Stations 3, 4, and 5. It acts as a routing mechanism, passing high-confidence claims directly to the graph while halting low-confidence claims for Human-In-The-Loop (HITL) review. Crucially, the human review process is designed to capture *why* the model failed, immediately producing high-quality training pairs to fine-tune the factory.

---

## 1. Task A: The Confidence Router

When a batch of `Knowledge Claims` arrives, the Router analyzes the `Confidence` value of *each individual claim* independently.

- **Auto-Approval:** If `Confidence >= 0.95`, the claim's `VerificationStatus` is immediately set to `Approved`. It bypasses the queue and is sent to Station 7 (Graph Construction).
- **Manual Review:** If `Confidence < 0.95`, the claim is halted. Its `VerificationStatus` remains `Pending`, and it is assigned to a human reviewer's queue.

Because confidence is per-claim, an image is never entirely discarded. If the system is 99% sure it's a Saree, but only 72% sure it is Banarasi, only the "Banarasi" claim halts.

---

## 2. Task B: The Human-in-the-Loop Interface

The reviewer (e.g., a Fashion Expert) opens their dashboard. The interface presents a highly structured view to facilitate rapid, accurate decision-making.

### The UI Displays:
1. **The Visual Context:** The original image, overlaid with the specific `SourceRegion` (bounding box/mask) that generated the claim.
2. **The Claim:** `Subject: Weave`, `Predicate: Technique`, `Value: Banarasi`.
3. **The Evidence:** The critical payload generated by the micro-model (e.g., `["Heavy gold zari border", "Floral brocade motifs"]`).
4. **The Model:** `ProducingModel: vif-textile-texture-v2`.

By seeing the *Evidence*, the reviewer understands the model's logic. They aren't just guessing if the model is right; they are evaluating the model's reasoning.

---

## 3. Task C: Evidence-Based Correction

The reviewer has three possible actions:

### Action 1: Approve
- The reviewer agrees with both the `Value` and the `Evidence`.
- **Outcome:** `VerificationStatus` becomes `Approved`. The claim moves to Station 7.

### Action 2: Correct with Reason (The Learning Loop)
- The reviewer disagrees with the `Value`. They realize the border actually indicates Kanjeevaram.
- The reviewer changes the `Value` to `Kanjeevaram` and inputs the correct `Evidence` (e.g., `["Contrast Korvai border"]`).
- **Outcome:** `VerificationStatus` becomes `Corrected`. The claim moves to Station 7.
- **Bonus Outcome:** A structured training pair is immediately dispatched to **Track C (Evaluation Platform)** to fine-tune `vif-textile-texture-v2`.

### Action 3: Reject (Ruthless Pruning)
- The image is too blurry, the garment is occluded, or the reviewer is genuinely unsure.
- **Outcome:** The claim is instantly destroyed. It never enters the Knowledge Graph.
- *Principle:* A smaller, 100% verified graph is infinitely superior to a massive graph containing assumptions.

---

## 4. Architectural Boundaries

- **Single Point of Entry:** No claim can enter the Knowledge Graph (Station 7) unless its `VerificationStatus` is `Approved` or `Corrected`.
- **Review is Asynchronous:** The rest of the factory does not block while waiting for a human. Claims sit safely in the queue until an expert logs in.
- **No Free-Text Tags:** The human reviewer cannot invent new tags on the fly. They are strictly bound to the limits of the `Universal` and `Domain` ontologies (`VIO-005`, `VIO-006`).
