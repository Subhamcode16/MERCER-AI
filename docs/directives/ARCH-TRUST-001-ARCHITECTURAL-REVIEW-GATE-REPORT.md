## D. Required Architectural Answers (Section 9 - Revised & Corrected)

1.  **What does the system actually know?**  
    The system does not possess objective knowledge. Its database records the current epistemic states of registered claims (representing that evidence currently satisfies defined verification conditions within declared scope, policy, and trust assumptions) and the chronological, chained sequence of ledger entries.
2.  **What evidence establishes that knowledge?**  
    Provenance-bound, cryptographically signed `Evidence` payloads and verifier run records generated by the isolated Verification Domain, complemented by nonce-checked NTP assertions. Cryptographic signatures establish origin authenticity, not objective truth.
3.  **Who is allowed to verify the evidence?**  
    The isolated `Verification Domain` (`VerificationHarness` and verifiers) running independently of generative intelligence models.
4.  **Who is allowed to authorize an action?**  
    Authority is separated into three distinct concepts:
    *   **Policy Definition:** Offline Administrative Keys / Security Policy Configuration.
    *   **Authorization Decision:** Decided by the `Assurance Domain` (`AssuranceLoopController` state matrix).
    *   **Enforcement:** Enforced by the `ExecutionGate` (which has zero authority to define policy or decide authorization, only to enforce).
5.  **Who can challenge the verification?**  
    Challenge authority is non-exclusive and multi-faceted. Challenges can originate from: the `Monitoring Domain` (`TelemetryMonitor`), new verification runs, counterexample discovery, temporal invalidations, policy changes, trust-anchor disagreements, manual investigation, and future adversarial verifiers.
6.  **Who can revoke the assurance?**  
    The `Assurance Domain` propagating invalidations (such as when a `Counterexample` is verified or telemetry goes offline).
7.  **Who can authorize recovery?**  
    The `Recovery Authority` (offline administrative keys) presenting signed override payloads matching the active `incident_uuid`, genesis hash, and block height.
8.  **What remains trustworthy when the subject itself is compromised?**  
    The declared architectural trust assumptions (`ASSUMED`): the host CPU monotonic clock, the decoupled `ExternalTrustAnchorService` consensus registry, and the offline administrative keys.
9.  **What happens when no trustworthy authority remains?**  
    The system fails-closed: all claims drop to `UNKNOWN` or `BLOCKED` states, locking out all privileged operations.
10. **Where does the architecture intentionally stop making claims?**  
    At the local physical hardware, host kernel/OS isolation, and offline administrative-key custody boundaries. The architecture explicitly classifies these as declared assumptions (`ASSUMED`) rather than proven trust roots.
