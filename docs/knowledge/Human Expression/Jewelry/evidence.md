# Evidence Record: Precious Metal Specular & Reflection Mapping

## 1. Source Details
Compiled from optical physics properties of metals (gold, platinum) and high-end jewelry rendering textbooks.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "Precious metals (yellow gold, rose gold, white gold, platinum) act as near-perfect mirrors, displaying high specular reflectivity (80-95%) with narrow specular width. Their appearance is almost entirely defined by reflection mapping—they reflect the shapes and colors of the surrounding environment. In a blank or poorly mapped environment, polished gold appears as flat, muddy yellow paint. White gold and platinum reflect neutral highlights, whereas yellow and rose gold tint the reflections with warm color values."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Optics):** Polished gold and platinum display mirror-like reflections that reflect surrounding environment geometries (reflection mapping). -> Promoted to KU-JEWEL-001
- **Claim 2 (Optics):** Warm golds (yellow, rose) tint reflections with their respective metallic color values, while platinum maintains neutral reflection tones. -> Promoted to KU-JEWEL-002

## 4. Context & Limitations
Brushed or matte jewelry metals bypass mirror reflection, showing broad diffuse sheen.

# Evidence Record: Gemstone Refraction and Dispersion (Fire)

## 1. Source Details
Extracted from gemological grading standards for cut diamonds and precious colored gems.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "A cut diamond has a high refractive index of 2.42 and a dispersion rating of 0.044. When light enters, it bends sharply, bounces off pavilion facets (internal reflections), and exits the crown split into spectral colors (red, orange, blue, violet), creating 'fire.' Renders that display gemstones as transparent colored glass with no internal reflections or rainbow dispersion read as cheap fake gems."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Optics):** Cut diamonds display sharp, complex internal facet reflections and rainbow-like dispersion (fire) highlights under directional light. -> Promoted to KU-JEWEL-003
- **Claim 2 (Optics):** Gemstones must be rendered with high refractive indices, sharp geometric facet boundaries, and clean transparency. -> Promoted to KU-JEWEL-004

## 4. Context & Limitations
Opaque stones like turquoise or lapis lazuli exhibit diffuse surface absorption rather than internal refraction/dispersion.

# Evidence Record: Fresnel Spot vs Diffused Studio Panels

## 1. Source Details
Interviews with luxury jewelry photographers and watch studio lighting layouts.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "Photographing jewelry requires a dual lighting approach. First, we map large, white diffused panels (light boxes) onto the metal surfaces to create clean, smooth specular bands along the shank/band. Second, we introduce small, hard Fresnel spot lights at a 45-degree angle to strike the gemstone facets. This hard directional light activates the internal refraction and creates the signature 'sparkle' or fire. Using soft light alone results in flat stones; using hard light alone creates harsh, blown-out metal glare."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Lighting):** Jewelry requires a combination of soft diffused panel lighting (for clean metal reflections) and hard Fresnel spot key lights (to trigger gemstone sparkle). -> Promoted to KU-JEWEL-005

## 4. Context & Limitations
Applies to faceted gem jewelry. Plain metal bands or pearls do not require Fresnel spots and rely entirely on diffused panel mapping.

# Evidence Record: Macro Lens & Razor-Thin DOF

## 1. Source Details
Macro photography rules and depth-of-field physical calculations.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "Due to the small physical size of jewelry (rings: 20-30mm), capturing them requires macro lenses (90mm-105mm macro) at close working distances. This close proximity creates a razor-thin depth-of-field, even at moderate apertures. The front edge of a ring may be tack-sharp while the back curve falls into soft, creamy bokeh. Renders that show the entire ring, hand, and background in sharp focus fail to communicate macro-photographic reality."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Camera):** Jewelry photography requires macro lens properties (90mm-105mm) with shallow depth-of-field, causing parts of the jewelry piece and the background to roll off into soft bokeh. -> Promoted to KU-JEWEL-006

## 4. Context & Limitations
Applies strictly to close-up jewelry catalog or detail shots. Model lifestyle shots (where jewelry is secondary to a pose) use standard portrait lenses.
