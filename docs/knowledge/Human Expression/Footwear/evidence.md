# Evidence Record: Polished Leather Reflection and Creases

## 1. Source Details
Compiled from luxury leathercraft standards and commercial shoe photography guides.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "High-grade polished leather has an exceptionally smooth surface finish resulting in narrow, sharp specular highlights. Along the flex zone of the toe box (vamp), continuous wear creates fine horizontal creasing. These wrinkles are shallow, organic, and interrupt the polished specular sheen. If the flex zone remains perfectly smooth in a walking pose, the render reads as synthetic plastic rather than natural leather."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Optics):** Polished leather requires narrow, sharp specular highlights to reflect light accurately. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-001
- **Claim 2 (Physics):** Walking or standing poses must display fine, organic horizontal creasing along the toe box vamp to read as worn leather. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-002

## 4. Context & Limitations
Does not apply to patent leather (which has a plastic finish with zero wrinkling) or raw nubuck.

# Evidence Record: Suede Pile and Light Absorption

## 1. Source Details
Extracted from physical testing of suede/nubuck materials under directional studio lighting.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "Suede consists of a napped pile surface of tiny fibers. It absorbs light in all directions, rendering it almost fully diffuse with a rich matte finish. Where the pile fibers are brushed in different directions (often caused by contact or styling), there is a distinct shift in color value: brushed pile appears lighter, and open/unbrushed pile appears darker. Flat front-lighting washes out this napped texture, making the suede look like flat card or felt."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Optics):** Suede is a fully diffuse matte surface that exhibits directional color value shifts (light/dark napped grain) based on pile orientation. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-003
- **Claim 2 (Lighting):** Harsher, angled key lighting is required to bring out the micro-shadows of the napped suede pile. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-004

## 4. Context & Limitations
Heavily treated waterproof suedes may show a flatter, less dynamic napped grain.

# Evidence Record: Lacing Symmetry and Eyelet Integrity

## 1. Source Details
Visual audits of AI-generated footwear campaigns and e-commerce shoe photography rules.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "The most common AI hallucination in footwear is the lacing system. Left and right shoes must have matching eyelet counts and symmetrical criss-cross lacing patterns. Shoelaces must drop naturally downward from the top eyelets under gravity, casting a drop shadow onto the tongue. Metal or plastic aglets must cap the lace tips."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Construction):** Symmetrical eyelet alignment and matching criss-cross lacing patterns are required between left and right shoes. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-005
- **Claim 2 (Physics):** Shoelaces must hang naturally downward under gravity and cast a visible drop shadow on the shoe tongue. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-006

## 4. Context & Limitations
Laceless footwear (slip-ons, Chelsea boots, Monk straps) bypasses these constraints.

# Evidence Record: Sole Grounding & Shadows

## 1. Source Details
Compiled from product rendering principles and photography ambient occlusion studies.

## 2. Raw Data / Quote
> "Footwear must never hover. A shoe in contact with the ground casts a dense contact shadow (ambient occlusion) directly under the outsole where tread blocks meet the floor. The shadow is pitch-black at the contact points and diffuses rapidly outward. The outsole tread profile (grips, lugs) must remain crisp, flat, and sharp along the contact line."

## 3. Extracted Claims
- **Claim 1 (Physics):** A shoe sitting on a surface must cast a high-density, black contact shadow directly under the outsole interface to establish grounding. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-007
- **Claim 2 (Construction):** Outsole tread patterns and lugs must remain crisp and sharp at the floor boundary, avoiding warped or melting edges. -> Promoted to KU-FOOT-008

## 4. Context & Limitations
Action shots (jumping, running) require the trailing shoe to cast a wider, highly diffused shadow matching the distance from the floor.
