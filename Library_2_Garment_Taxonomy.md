# Library 2 — Garment Taxonomy

This library teaches the AI **how garments are constructed**. It maps the structural components of each garment type in top-to-bottom order.

## Saree
An iconic, unstitched, and elegantly draped item of Indian dress.
* **Border:** The woven or embroidered edge of the saree, often featuring contrasting colors or heavy zari work.
* **Pallu:** The loose end of the saree that is draped over the shoulder, usually the most heavily decorated part.
* **Pleats:** The folded section of the saree tucked into the waist, providing structure and movement to the drape.
* **Body:** The main expanse of the fabric.
* **Fall:** A protective inner lining attached to the bottom hem of the saree body to add weight and prevent fraying.
* **Blouse:** The tailored upper garment worn with the saree.
* **Tassels:** Decorative elements sometimes added to the end of the pallu.

## Kurti
A tailored tunic-like upper garment.
* **Neckline:** The cut and design around the neck (e.g., V-neck, sweetheart, mandarin collar).
* **Sleeves:** The arm coverings, varying in length and fit.
* **Hemline:** The bottom edge of the kurti, which can be straight, asymmetrical, or flared.
* **Fabric:** The primary material used.
* **Length:** The overall vertical measurement (e.g., knee-length, floor-length).
* **Fit:** The silhouette and tailoring (e.g., A-line, straight, Anarkali).
* **Embroidery:** Decorative thread work or embellishments.

## Lehenga
A traditional wedding or festive skirt ensemble.
* **Skirt (Lehenga):** The voluminous, floor-length skirt.
* **Can-can:** A stiff mesh layer attached under the skirt to give it flare and volume.
* **Dupatta:** The long scarf or veil draped over the shoulder or head.
* **Blouse (Choli):** The fitted, cropped upper garment.
* **Border:** The decorative edging at the bottom hem of the skirt and dupatta.
* **Flare:** The circumference and volume of the skirt (measured in meters).
