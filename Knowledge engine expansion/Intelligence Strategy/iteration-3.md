Research Question 2 — Iteration 3
=================================

New Objective
-------------

We're no longer optimizing for technical compatibility.

We're optimizing for:

> **Technical Fit × Market Opportunity × Customer Willingness to Pay**

A product category is only a winner if it scores highly in all three.

Step 1 — What do successful US DTC brands spend money on?
=========================================================

After reviewing current ecommerce and DTC trends, one pattern appears repeatedly:

The bottleneck is **not taking one great product photo**.

The bottleneck is producing **continuous, on-brand creative content** for every channel—Shopify, Meta, TikTok, email, landing pages, seasonal launches, and product variants.

That is an excellent match for your platform.

Step 2 — Which industries have the highest "creative pressure"?
===============================================================

Instead of asking which industries are largest, let's ask:

> Which industries need fresh campaigns every week?

I call this **Creative Pressure**.

Tier 1 — Extremely High Creative Pressure
-----------------------------------------

### Fashion & Apparel ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Needs:

*   New collections
    
*   Seasonal campaigns
    
*   Lifestyle shoots
    
*   Social media
    
*   Influencer creatives
    
*   Paid ads
    

Products are highly visual.

Creative quality directly affects sales.

**This is nearly a perfect fit.**

### Beauty & Skincare ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Needs:

*   Product launches
    
*   Ingredient education
    
*   Influencer content
    
*   Lifestyle imagery
    
*   UGC-style campaigns
    

However...

Beauty relies heavily on claims that aren't visible in a product image (ingredients, skin benefits, routines).

So commercially attractive...

…but technically harder.

### Jewelry ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Needs:

*   Luxury storytelling
    
*   Editorial campaigns
    
*   Gift campaigns
    
*   Holiday promotions
    

Very high visual appeal.

Your material and lighting solver already aligns well here.

Tier 2 — High Creative Pressure
===============================

### Home & Living

Products:

*   Furniture
    
*   Home décor
    
*   Lighting
    
*   Ceramics
    

Needs:

*   Room scenes
    
*   Lifestyle environments
    
*   Seasonal styling
    

Excellent for scene generation.

### Footwear

Interesting discovery.

Shoes behave much more like fashion than electronics.

The product tells a rich visual story.

Campaigns change constantly.

Strong expansion candidate.

### Bags & Accessories

High visual information.

Luxury positioning.

Strong social media demand.

Minimal reasoning complexity.

Tier 3 — Moderate Creative Pressure
===================================

### Consumer Electronics

Needs campaigns.

But...

Customers also buy based on:

*   battery life
    
*   chipset
    
*   specifications
    
*   compatibility
    

Those cannot be inferred from images.

Technology is only part of the buying decision.

### Food

Needs branding.

But taste, aroma, freshness and ingredients dominate purchasing decisions.

Those are invisible.

Step 4 — A New Discovery
========================

I think we've been thinking in terms of **product categories**.

I think the real expansion unit is something else.

It's **visual domains**.

For example:

Domain A — Wearables
--------------------

Includes:

*   Sarees
    
*   Dresses
    
*   Hoodies
    
*   Shoes
    
*   Bags
    
*   Hats
    
*   Jewelry
    

Shared intelligence:

*   Fashion
    
*   Styling
    
*   Editorial photography
    
*   Materials
    
*   Lifestyle
    
*   Human models
    

One solver.

One ontology.

Many products.

Domain B — Living Spaces
------------------------

Includes:

*   Furniture
    
*   Lamps
    
*   Rugs
    
*   Home décor
    
*   Ceramics
    

Shared intelligence:

*   Interior design
    
*   Lighting
    
*   Room composition
    
*   Architecture
    
*   Spatial reasoning
    

Domain C — Beauty
-----------------

Includes:

*   Makeup
    
*   Skincare
    
*   Haircare
    
*   Fragrance
    

Requires:

*   Skin science
    
*   Beauty routines
    
*   Ingredient reasoning
    
*   Cosmetic regulations
    

Almost an entirely new intelligence module.

This is a much cleaner roadmap than expanding one product category at a time.

Step 5 — A Business Insight
===========================

Suppose two companies each sell 50 products.

Company A:

50 T-shirts.

Company B:

25 T-shirts

10 Hoodies

5 Shoes

5 Bags

5 Hats

Our platform should not care.

Because all of these belong to the same **Wearables Intelligence Domain**.

That means a fashion brand doesn't buy "T-shirt Intelligence."

They buy **Fashion Intelligence**.

That's a much stronger product strategy.

Biggest Breakthrough of Iteration 3
===================================

This is the moment I think our roadmap changed.

Your backend shouldn't become:

> "A collection of product-specific models."

It should become:

> **A collection of domain-specific intelligence systems.**

That aligns perfectly with the architecture we've already designed.

The Product Intelligence module stays universal.

The domain knowledge becomes plug-ins.

Self-review
-----------

I think this is around **9.6/10**.

The only thing left before we lock Question 2 is the **founder's decision**:

Should Version 2 of the platform expand by **product category** or by **intelligence domain**?

At this point, my recommendation is clear:

**Expand by intelligence domains.**

It keeps the architecture cleaner, allows you to serve more customers with each expansion, and better matches how real brands organize their product catalogs.
