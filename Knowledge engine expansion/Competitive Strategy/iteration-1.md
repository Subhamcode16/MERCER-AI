RES-003 — Iteration 1
=====================

Mapping the Competitive Landscape
---------------------------------

The biggest mistake founders make is saying:

> "Our competitors are Midjourney."

They aren't.

Because customers aren't buying "AI."

They're trying to complete a workflow.

Category 1 — Image Generation Models
------------------------------------

Examples

*   GPT Image
    
*   Midjourney
    
*   Flux
    
*   Ideogram
    

### Their Job

Generate images.

They are incredibly good execution engines.

Strengths

*   Beautiful visuals
    
*   Flexible prompting
    
*   Fast iteration
    
*   General purpose
    

Weaknesses

They don't know:

*   your brand,
    
*   your campaign,
    
*   your customer,
    
*   your product strategy.
    

The user provides all of that.

**Conclusion**

These are rendering engines, not creative systems.

Category 2 — Product Photography AI
-----------------------------------

Examples

*   Photoroom
    
*   Flair AI
    
*   Pebblely
    

### Their Job

Turn existing product photos into ecommerce-ready images.

They focus on:

*   background replacement,
    
*   lifestyle scenes,
    
*   studio photography,
    
*   simple ad creatives.
    

Strengths

*   Fast
    
*   Ecommerce-focused
    
*   Easy to use
    
*   Product-aware
    

Weaknesses

They still assume the user already knows:

*   what campaign to create,
    
*   what message to communicate,
    
*   which audience they're targeting.
    

They improve execution, not creative strategy.

Category 3 — Design Platforms
-----------------------------

Examples

*   Canva
    
*   Adobe Express
    

### Their Job

Help users assemble and publish marketing assets.

Strengths

*   Templates
    
*   Brand kits
    
*   Collaboration
    
*   Multi-channel publishing
    
*   Social media workflows
    
*   Brand consistency features
    

Weaknesses

They don't originate campaigns.

They help users build what they've already decided to create.

Category 4 — Creative Suites
----------------------------

Examples

*   Adobe Photoshop
    
*   Illustrator
    
*   Lightroom
    

### Their Job

Professional creative production.

Strengths

*   Maximum control
    
*   Professional editing
    
*   Industry standard
    

Weaknesses

They require expertise.

They don't provide creative reasoning.

Step 3 — The Industry's Hidden Assumption
=========================================

Every competitor assumes:

> The human already knows what to create.

That assumption appears everywhere.

Example:

**Midjourney**

"Write a prompt."

**GPT Image**

"Describe what you want."

**Flair**

"Build your scene."

**Canva**

"Choose a template."

Nobody asks

> Why are you creating this campaign?

Or

> What is your brand trying to communicate?

Step 4 — This Changes Our Category
==================================

I don't think we're competing with image generators.

We're competing with **creative decision making**.

Imagine two founders.

Founder A

Spends two hours writing prompts.

Founder B

Uploads a product.

The platform asks intelligent questions.

Produces

*   campaign strategy,
    
*   scene concepts,
    
*   creative direction,
    
*   and then renders.
    

That's a completely different workflow.

Biggest Discovery
=================

I think we've been describing the company incorrectly.

We've been saying:

> "Creative Intelligence Platform."

That's true.

But it's still abstract.

A simpler way to express it is:

> **We automate the work of a creative director before automating the work of a designer.**

That's a sentence investors, customers, and employees can understand immediately.

Self Review
===========

I think this is a **9.3/10**.

Why not higher?

Because we still need to validate one critical assumption:

> **Do customers actually spend more time deciding** _**what**_ **to create than generating the final assets?**

If the answer is yes, we've found our moat.

If the answer is no, we need to rethink our positioning.

I suspect the answer is yes—but I don't want to assume it. I think **Iteration 2** should follow a real customer journey, from a product arriving in the warehouse all the way to a campaign going live, and measure exactly where time, money, and decision-making are spent. That will tell us whether our "Creative Intelligence" layer solves the most painful part of the workflow.
